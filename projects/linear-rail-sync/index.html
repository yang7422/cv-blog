Synchronized Twin Linear Rails

Two belt rails, two ATmega128s: PID position control, a GPIO handshake, homing and fault recovery, Aug. 2020 –⁠ Feb. 2021

From Aug. 2020 to Feb. 2021, as an undergraduate researcher at IHLAB (Tech University of Korea), I developed the control for two belt-driven linear rails that had to move together. Each rail has its own DC gearmotor with an encoder and its own ATmega128, and the motors run on bought H-bridge drivers, so the two controllers have to coordinate over the wires between them. I tuned PID position control on one rail first, then took the pair through seven steps, from a scheme that did not synchronize to homing and fault recovery. Each step below is taken from a dated progress report of that period.

What this project shows

Measured, not assumed

I read the PID dt off the scope, 5.65 ms between pulses on a pin set once per loop, and counted 12141 encoder counts over the full 520 mm stroke. On the scope the PWM read 28.90 kHz, over a new driver's 20 kHz limit, until a timer change brought it to 18.07 kHz.

The cause before the gain

When the two rails stuttered, a lower gain only traded stutter for speed (Kp 200 against Kp 30). The log pointed to the handshake instead: the in-position window had to take in an error of 17.418 mm, or each rail slowed before every handover. A window of 0.6 × the segment (18 mm) made the motion continuous.

Dead ends on record

Streaming the master's position did not synchronize the rails, a UART lockstep stuttered, and a stop check on unchanged position was rejected (marked ✗ in its report). Each one stays in the reports next to what replaced it.

Safety in the wiring

Cross-wired GPIO lines between the two MCUs carry "in position" and "stop", and a limit switch on a pulled-up input homes each rail. On a fault both rails stop, wait 1 s and back off toward their limit switches.

1 · One rail first: PID position control

Aug. – Sep. 2020 · ATmega128, L298N H-bridge module, 12 V encoder gearmotors, a 520 mm belt rail

  • Drive and sense. In Aug. 2020 I drove a 12 V encoder gearmotor through an L298N H-bridge module with timer PWM and counted its hall-encoder pulses on an external interrupt. Tuning PID angle control one gain at a time brought the peak from 216° down to 180.78° for a 180° target.
  • One belt rail under PID. On a 520 mm belt rail I counted 12141 encoder counts over the full stroke (0.04283 mm per count) and pulsed a spare pin once per pass of the control loop: the scope cursors read 5.65 ms between pulses, which became the PID dt. Sweeping P, PD, PI and PID gains one at a time, Kp 7, Ki 0.1 and Kd 0.7 came closest: 99.794 mm for a 100 mm target.
  • The scope before a guess. When a bench gearmotor ran slowly at 60% PWM, I put the signal on the scope with and without a capacitor. Without it the rising edges smeared; with it connected, the motor ran fast.

2 · Two rails: from streaming to a hardware handshake

Nov. 2020 – Jan. 2021 · steps 1–5 · one ATmega128 per rail, 24 V encoder gearmotors, bought H-bridge drivers

  • Steps 1–2: stream, then share a trajectory. In Nov. 2020 each rail got a 24 V gearmotor with a 512-pulse encoder (0.002447 mm per count), a bought 20 A H-bridge board and its own ATmega128. Streaming the master’s position to the slave did not synchronize the rails, so both controllers instead cut the move into segments of one shared trajectory and stepped the setpoint every 10 ms.
  • Step 3: a UART lockstep that stuttered. At a fixed interval each MCU sent “@” (segment reached) or “/” (not yet), and neither moved on until both had arrived. The rails stuttered and crawled. A speed sketch in the report gives the cause: waiting for the other side’s confirmation made each rail ramp down to a stop at every segment target and back up again.
  • Step 4: a GPIO handshake with the right window. I moved the “in position” signal to a dedicated GPIO line, modeled on the in-position (INP) output of servo drives, with a ±0.1 mm window, but the rails still stuttered at Kp 200, and Kp 30 was smooth but slower. The log put a number on the problem: the window had to take in an error of 17.418 mm, or each rail slowed before every handover. A window of 0.6 × the segment length (18 mm for 30 mm segments) covers it, so the handover comes while the drive is still at full command, and the motion became continuous.
  • Step 5: stop on a speed drop, and stop both. A stop check on unchanged position was rejected; the report marks it ✗. Instead I estimated speed every 10 ms in a timer interrupt: a 300 mm move cruised near 0.19 m/s and ended at 300.122 mm. A rail that slows under load after reaching cruise now cuts its PWM and raises a stop line wired to the other MCU, which stops as well.

3 · Speed in m/s, PWM under the driver’s limit

Jan. 2021 · step 6 · speed calibration, oscilloscope, Timer0 fast PWM

  • Step 6: a drive command with units. I measured the rail’s speed at 16 PWM settings (OCR 10–160) and fitted a line, speed = 0.0036 × duty − 0.0115, with duty in % and speed in m/s. The position error could then be issued as a speed: OCR = (err + 0.0115) / 0.0036 × 2.55.
  • PWM re-timed for a new driver. To match the two motors, I planned a new H-bridge driver. Its datasheet allows PWM up to 20 kHz, but my phase-correct PWM read 28.90 kHz on the scope. Switching Timer0 to fast PWM, with the counter reloaded to 155 in the overflow interrupt, brought it to 18.07 kHz on the scope’s frequency counter.

4 · Homing, fault recovery and the final run

Feb. 2021 · step 7 · limit switches, GPIO fault lines

  • Step 7: homing. Before the first command, each rail runs backward at OCR0 30 until its limit switch closes; the switch input idles high through a pull-up. The MCU then zeroes its position and encoder counters and starts the controller.
  • Fault recovery. A rail whose speed drops while it is still short of its target stops and raises its fault line, and a rail that sees the other’s fault line stops too. After 1 s both back off at OCR0 40, and a switch on INT0 resets every variable for the next run.
  • Wiring. Between the two ATmega128s I cross-wired GPIO lines for “in position” and “stop”, and each rail’s limit switch sits on a pulled-up input.
  • Not measured. The reports log each rail’s own position and error, not the offset between the two carriages, so this page claims no synchronization accuracy.

한국어 요약

2020년 8월부터 2021년 2월까지 IHLAB 학부연구생으로서 벨트 구동 리니어 레일 두 개가 함께 움직이도록 제어를 개발했습니다. 레일마다 엔코더가 달린 DC 기어모터와 ATmega128을 따로 두었고, 모터는 시판 H-브리지 드라이버로 구동했습니다. 먼저 레일 하나의 PID 위치제어로 100 mm 목표에 99.794 mm까지 맞췄고, 이어서 위치 스트리밍(동기화 실패), 구간 궤적, UART 락스텝, GPIO 핸드셰이크와 인포지션 허용범위, 속도 저하 정지, 듀티 대비 속도 보정과 PWM 재설정, 리밋 스위치 원점 복귀와 고장 복구까지 일곱 단계를 거쳤습니다.

버벅임은 게인을 낮추는 대신 원인을 찾아 해결했습니다. 로그를 보니 인포지션 허용범위가 17.418 mm의 오차를 받아들이지 못해 레일이 구간마다 감속하고 있었고, 허용범위를 구간 길이의 0.6배(18 mm)로 넓히자 레일이 끊김 없이 움직였습니다. 새 드라이버의 20 kHz 제한에 맞춰 PWM을 28.90 kHz에서 18.07 kHz로 낮추고 오실로스코프로 확인했습니다. 보고서에는 레일별 위치와 오차만 있어서 두 레일 사이의 동기 정밀도는 수치로 제시하지 않습니다.
