Robotics from the Ground Up

Weekly undergraduate lab seminars, Dec. 2019 – Jul. 2021: servo packets built by hand, a legged robot, and a robot that follows a person

Before graduate school, I spent 19 months as an undergraduate researcher at IHLAB (Tech University of Korea) and gave a lab seminar almost every week. Each one covered what I had planned, built and measured, what broke, and what I would fix by the next week. The work ran from a single servo on a bare microcontroller to a robot that follows a person. This page condenses that archive into the parts that still describe how I work.

How I work

Start at the wire

I wrote the low-level code myself: DYNAMIXEL packets byte by byte on an ATmega128, and an nRF24L01+ radio driver at register level. I checked the traffic on an oscilloscope or a logic analyzer.

Measure against ground truth

Foot positions were traced on paper, ranges were taped out on a floor grid, and radio timing was logged over 519 exchanges. A number only counted once it had been checked.

Fix causes, not symptoms

When a filter would only have hidden a problem, I kept digging. Range spikes turned out to be reflections, timing jitter a slow status-register read, and a silent radio a CE pin that was never raised.

Write it all down, dead ends included

Every talk opens with the plan and ends with open questions. "Not solved yet" appears often, and so does a wide-angle receiver I could not make work, measured and explained rather than quietly dropped.

1 · Servo packets to a legged robot

Dec. 2019 – Mar. 2020 · ATmega128, DYNAMIXEL AX-12A / RX-64 / EX-106+, RS-485

  • Protocol by hand. I drove DYNAMIXEL servos from a bare ATmega128. The half-duplex line switching, the instruction packets and their checksums were written byte by byte. I parsed the status packets, checked them on the oscilloscope, and then moved several servos at once with Sync Write.
  • From kinematics to a walking robot. I derived forward and inverse kinematics for a 3-axis arm, first as a 2-D problem and then with the DH convention. The same 3-DOF leg, on RX-64 and EX-106+ servos over RS-485, became the leg of a legged “spider” robot that walks and turns.
  • Measured, then redesigned. I traced foot positions on paper and compared them with the computed ones (2–5 mm apart). A leg that should keep its stride drifted by 25 mm, so I replaced joint-angle steps with Cartesian interpolation and inverse kinematics at every small step.

2 · Following unit: ultrasonic ranging and my own boards

Mar. – Nov. 2020 · ATmega128 → STM32 (Cortex-M4), ultrasonic transducers, nRF24L01+, OrCAD

  • Sensors measured before they were chosen. The robot would follow a person who carries a small transmitter. A radio message marks the moment a 40 kHz ultrasonic burst leaves, so each receiver measures a one-way time of flight, and the law of cosines turns two or three ranges into distance and bearing. I tested each candidate sensor against a tape measure. One reached 1 cm and 1.6° in three-sensor triangulation, but I still dropped it: at 70 ms per reading and a 30° beam, it could not meet the 30 ms target.
  • Causes, not filters. Range spikes during fast motion survived a low-pass filter, so I kept looking and found reflections. A sweep of drive voltage against acceptance angle settled on 9 V. Once the spikes were gone, the smoothing filter came out again, because it only added lag.
  • My own boards and drivers. I laid out two boards in OrCAD, with trace widths and vias sized for current, and wrote a 26-slide PCB manual along the way. I brought the boards up on the bench and wrote a register-level nRF24L01+ driver, first on the ATmega128 and then on an STM32. End-to-end tuning cut the trigger skew between the boards from 424 to 84 µs and the update period from 50 to 35 ms.

3 · Graduation project: a person-following robot

Dec. 2020 – Jul. 2021 · a team of three; my part was the following unit · STM32F429 / F405, nRF24L01+, SRF02

  • The system. The goal was a tracked transport robot for construction sites that follows a worker carrying a handheld target. My teammates handled the drive (motors and IMU) and camera-based target detection. I built the following unit: the radio-synchronized ultrasonic localization, its boards, and the handheld target.
  • Localization that held up under measurement. Four receivers on a 45 × 20 cm frame locate the target every 29 ms. After I re-mounted the receivers at an outward angle, the error stayed within 3.6 cm and 2° up to 40° off-axis at 1–1.5 m. A lost radio acknowledgment delayed the burst by about 2.5 ms, which is roughly 85 cm of range error. I turned off auto-retransmit and added a resend timeout and a 70 cm jump filter, then checked the result over 519 exchanges.
  • Boards, and a dead end on record. The following-control board (STM32F429, 80.8 × 74.4 mm) has four ultrasonic UARTs, four DYNAMIXEL ports, a CAN IMU, radio, Bluetooth and six infrared inputs. A 90° wide-angle receiver never worked with our transmitter. I measured why (57 vs. 3.8 Vpp drive, different attenuation) and wrote it up.

Debugging log

Some of the faults I tracked down, and how I found them.

When Symptom How I found it Fix
Jan. 2020 Delays ran about 15× too long on a new controller board Back-calculated the real clock (about 1 MHz instead of 14.7456 MHz). Measured both crystals on the scope and checked the fuse bits. Replaced the MCU: the first chip or its soldering was bad.
Feb. 2020 Servo angles read back as garbage Probed the RS-485 transceiver. The receive line floats whenever the driver is off. Added a 10 kΩ pull-up and a termination resistor, and confirmed with before and after captures.
May 2020 Range spikes during fast motion that a low-pass filter could not remove Checked the received frequency, then traced the spikes to reflections. Swept drive voltage against acceptance angle. Drove the transmitter at 9 V and removed the filter.
Sep. 2020 Radio latency jittered between 9.5 and 10.5 ms Timed each step of the loop. Reading the status register alone took 2.73 ms. Shortened the loop. Latency became 6.00–6.02 ms and is now a constant offset.
Jan. 2021 The radio worked on my own board but stayed silent on a dev board Compared SPI traffic on a logic analyzer, matched the register writes, and checked every register against the datasheet, one change at a time. The datasheet's state diagram showed CE was never raised. Driving CE fixed it.
Feb. 2021 A target standing still at 70 cm sometimes read about 210 cm Timed the radio handshake on the scope. Each lost packet or acknowledgment delays the burst by 2.5–3 ms. Turned off auto-retransmit and added a resend timeout and a 70 cm jump filter. Checked over 519 exchanges.
May 2021 A 90° wide-angle receiver gave erratic data Measured both transmitters (57 vs. 3.8 Vpp) and matched echo amplitudes by distance. It worked only at 25–45 cm. Recorded it as a dead end and kept the original receivers.

한국어 요약

한국공학대학교 IHLAB 학부연구생 시절(2019.12–2021.07)에 거의 매주 진행한 랩 세미나 61회를 요약한 페이지입니다. ATmega128에서 다이나믹셀 패킷을 한 바이트씩 직접 만들고 오실로스코프로 확인하는 데서 시작해, 기구학으로 다리 로봇을 걷게 했습니다. 이어서 초음파와 RF 동기화로 사람의 위치를 재는 추종부를 직접 설계한 STM32 보드로 만들었고, 이 작업은 사람을 따라가는 공사장 자재 운반 로봇(졸업작품)으로 이어졌습니다.

모든 결과는 줄자·각도기·오실로스코프로 실측과 비교했습니다. 문제는 필터로 덮기보다 원인(반사파, 느린 레지스터 읽기, 올라가지 않은 CE 핀)을 찾아 고쳤고, 실패와 막다른 길까지 매주 기록했습니다.
