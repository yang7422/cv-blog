Person‑Following Robot and Its Boards

Radio‑synced ultrasonic ranging on boards I designed: two assembled by hand and brought up, three laid out (Mar. 2020 –⁠ Jul. 2021)

As an undergraduate researcher at IHLAB (Tech University of Korea), I developed the sensing side of a robot that follows a person, from Mar. 2020 to Jul. 2021. The person carries a small transmitter, and a radio message tells the robot when its 40 kHz ultrasonic burst leaves, so every receiver on the robot times a one-way flight. In 2020 this following unit was my own project, including two boards I designed, assembled by hand once the bare PCBs arrived, and brought up. In 2021 it became my part of a three-person graduation project, a material-transport robot for construction sites, and I laid out three more boards for it.

What this project shows

Circuits I design, assemble and bring up

Two 2-layer STM32F405 boards drawn in OrCAD in Jul. 2020 and soldered by hand, with traces sized by IPC-2221 and vias enlarged for current. At bring-up I found a missing ISP supply and an LDO dropout, and scoped the regulator input in four capacitor combinations.

Measured against ground truth

Targets stood at set distances and angles, on paper laid out on the floor or a hand-drawn 10 cm grid board, and timing came from two-channel scope captures. After I re-mounted the receivers, every point from 0 to 40° at 100 and 150 cm read within 3.6 cm and 2°.

Causes before filters

Range spikes turned out to be reflections; once a lower drive voltage removed them, the median filter came out again. Latency jitter traced to a polling loop slowed by a debug print, a silent radio was a CE pin never raised, and 2.29 ms of command overhead explained ≈78 of an 82 cm offset.

Dead ends on record

Even with echo amplitudes matched, a 90° wide-angle receiver returned data only at 25–45 cm with our transmitter. I measured why (≈57 vs. ≈3.8 Vpp drive, different attenuation) and kept the old receivers. Vertical localization failed beyond about 10°, and the three 2021 boards stayed layouts.

Boards I designed

  • Two boards, designed, hand-assembled and brought up. In Jul. 2020 I analyzed the lab’s earlier RF board and a revision of its power and radio sections, then drew the robot-side receiver board in OrCAD Capture and OrCAD Layout, through at least four layout versions; a handheld transmitter board followed. Both are 2-layer boards around an STM32F405RG with an nRF24L01+ radio header; once the bare PCBs were fabricated, I soldered the parts on by hand. The robot-side board takes 24 V through a polyfuse and a series Schottky diode into an LM2576S-5 buck and an LM1117-3.3, and adds RS-485, a buzzer driver and four ultrasonic ports. I also compiled a 26-slide OrCAD guide: its layout rules are shown on my own boards, and 7 of its slides come from an external tutorial.
  • Sized for current, checked before fabrication. I sized the traces with an IPC-2221 calculator: 30.8 mil for 1 A at a 10 °C rise, its internal-layer result, which errs wide on a 2-layer board. I enlarged the vias from 0.762/0.381 to 1.000/0.700 mm pad/drill, which leaves exactly the fab house’s 0.15 mm minimum annular ring. After the lab’s review I renumbered a 10-pin header footprint that had been numbered like a DIP part.
  • Bring-up, measured. The handheld board’s ST-LINK would not attach because I had left target VCC off the ISP header; a bodge wire to MCU_VDD fixed it. Its 3.3 V rail read 2.8 V because the LM1117 needs at least 4.75 V and the battery gives 3.8 V, so I switched to an AME8801AEEV low-dropout regulator. On the robot-side board I fitted the buck converter’s input capacitors in four combinations and scoped the rail each time: only both together gave a clean rail.
  • Add-ons, then three more layouts. In Nov. 2020 I designed two perfboard add-ons for a wide-beam transducer: a single-transistor level shifter for its 4–5 V trigger input, and an LM2940CS-12 regulator soldered onto the robot-side board, because at 5 V the transducer fell far short of 3 m. For the graduation project I laid out three more boards by Jul. 2021; none of them was fabricated.

Ranging and the radio link (2020)

  • One-way ranging. A radio message announces each ultrasonic burst, so every receiver on the robot times a one-way flight, and two or three ranges give distance and bearing. I worked out how range error becomes angle error: with receivers 45 cm apart, ±1 cm of range error shifts the computed angles by 2.34° at 50 cm and 2.53° at 150 cm.
  • Sensors measured before they were chosen. I tested the candidates at set distances and angles. The HC-SR04 stayed within −1.08 to +0.99 % at 5–50 cm. Three SRF02 sensors triangulated within 1 cm and 1.6°, but I set them aside for the rest of 2020: over I²C a reading needed more than 65 ms (70 ms by the datasheet), and the incidence angle was only 30°. Before that, a constant 82 cm offset in one-way SRF02 ranging turned out to be mostly command overhead: the 2.29 ms between the listen and burst commands explains ≈78 cm of it.
  • Causes, not filters. Two transmitters gave unstable ranges, including a 35 µs echo-arrival jitter (1.19 cm), so I moved to one transmitter and several receivers. Range spikes from the HG-M40DB transducer during fast motion then survived both an RC low-pass filter I derived and a median filter. I traced them to reflections, swept the drive voltage against the acceptance angle and settled on 9 V (12 V spiked even with the robot standing still), then dropped the median filter, which only slowed the response.
  • The radio link, end to end. I wrote a register-level nRF24L01+ driver on two ATmega128s, pinned its latency at a constant 6.01 ms and ported it to STM32 with HAL. Tuning both sides together in Nov. 2020 cut the trigger skew from 428 to 84 µs and the sampling period from 49.9 to 34.0 ms, with ranges read out to 388 cm. Bearing stayed the weak point: a target at 40° read 53°, and the wide-beam transducer I tried next returned no echo 15° or more off-axis.

The graduation project (2021)

  • The team and my part. The goal was a tracked robot that carries materials up construction-site stairs at least 120 cm wide, following a worker. We were a team of three. The drive teammate took drive control, the landing gear, anti-tilt control and path planning; the third member took the manual remote software, terrain judgment and the cargo bay. I owned the following subsystem: its boards, the ultrasonic and radio localization, the receive-range extension and the LiDAR interface.
  • Localization, measured against marked positions. With two receivers on a bar, re-mounting them at an angle brought every point from 0 to 40° at 100 and 150 cm within 3.6 cm and 2°; before, a target at 30° and 100 cm read 120.2 cm and 44°. With four receivers, targets at 70 cm and 20, 30 and 40° to the side read 19.8, 32.1 and 42.1°, though the 10° point read 1.1°. Vertical localization broke down beyond about 10°: a target 40° up read 90° and 178 cm.
  • Radio failures handled, then counted. A target standing at 70 cm sometimes read about 210 cm. On the scope, a lost packet cost ≈3 ms and a lost acknowledgment ≈1.2 ms. I turned auto-retransmit off (15 → 0), resent after 1 ms whenever transmission took longer than 1.5 ms, and rejected readings that jumped by more than 70 cm. After the fix, each lost packet adds 3 ms to the 29 ms cycle. A lost acknowledgment still delays the burst by ≈2.5 ms (≈85 cm of range), so the jump filter drops that reading and the cycle stretches to ≈61 ms. Of 519 logged exchanges, 492 succeeded on the first try, 25 lost one packet, 1 lost two in a row and 1 lost the acknowledgment.
  • Dead ends, written down. A 90° wide-angle receiver (IUM-D10) gave erratic data with our transmitter. I measured both drives (≈57 vs. ≈3.8 Vpp), matched echo amplitudes by distance with a 1.765 ms delay, and still got data only at 25–45 cm, so I kept the SRF02 receivers. A DYNAMIXEL receiver tilt was dropped from the plan, though I kept its connectors on the July layout. The first following run in Apr. 2021 failed on spikes; the logs put them in the measured motor speeds, while the following unit’s commands, sent every 50 ms, stayed smooth.

한국어 요약

2020년 3월부터 2021년 7월까지 사람을 따라가는 로봇의 추종부를 맡았습니다. 사람이 든 송신기가 초음파를 쏘는 순간을 RF로 알려 주면, 로봇의 수신기가 단방향 비행시간으로 거리와 방향을 구하는 방식입니다. 2020년에는 이 추종부가 제 개인 과제였고, STM32F405 보드 두 장을 OrCAD로 설계하고, 제작된 기판에 부품을 직접 납땜해 조립한 뒤 브링업까지 했습니다. 2021년에는 3명이 함께한 졸업작품에서 건설 현장용 자재 운반 로봇의 추종 시스템을 맡아 보드 세 장을 더 설계해 레이아웃까지 마쳤지만, 제작하지는 않았습니다.

결과는 실측과 비교해 확인했습니다. 바닥에 깐 종이와 10 cm 격자판 위에서 목표 위치를 정하고 오실로스코프로 타이밍을 쟀으며, 수신기를 비스듬히 다시 단 뒤에는 100·150 cm에서 0–40° 전 구간이 거리 3.6 cm, 각도 2° 이내로 들어왔습니다. 무선 패킷 손실 처리는 519회 교환으로 확인했고(첫 시도 성공 492회), 문제는 필터로 덮기보다 반사파, 폴링 루프 안의 디버그 출력, 올라가지 않은 CE 핀처럼 원인을 찾아 고쳤습니다. 90° 광각 수신기 실패와 10° 이상에서 무너진 상하 위치 추정처럼 막힌 길도 그대로 기록했습니다.
